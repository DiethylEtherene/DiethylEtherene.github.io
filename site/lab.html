Food with Asu — a cookbook from saved videos

SEP – OCT 2026PERSONAL
  • PRODUCT DESIGN
  • AI-ASSISTED BUILD
  • WEB APP

My partner and I had saved over a hundred recipe videos across Instagram and Douyin and cooked almost none of them. The recipes were trapped in captions and comment threads, half of them in Chinese, with no quantities and no way to plan a week. Food with Asu turns them into a cookbook we actually cook from. Every dish has quantities, steps, tap-to-start timers and a nutrition estimate; the planner suggests a week around a health goal and flags the days that run low on fibre or high on salt; the shopping list knows what’s already in the cupboard. Paste a recipe link and it reads the caption into a recipe, swapping sponsored ingredients for supermarket ones. Anyone can use it: start a kitchen, share the code, and every phone in the house shows the same plan, live.

I designed it and directed the build with Claude Code — Claude wrote the code, I set the direction and made the design calls — and tested every version by cooking from it. Underneath it’s a single-page app on this site and a small Cloudflare backend that syncs each kitchen and doubles as a Claude connector, so Claude can read a link, write a recipe or plan the week straight into the app from a chat. Most of the design work came from using it. Toggling meal prep wiped the planned week, so the planner got an undo and never overwrites unless asked. Timers buried in step text were useless mid-stir, so any time in a step became tap-to-start. Saved recipes kept ‘vanishing’ because they were quietly stacking onto similar dishes, so stacks now look like stacks.

[02]

Bedside ASMR player

AUG 2026 – PRESENTSOLO · IN PROGRESS
  • MECHANISM DESIGN
  • CLOSED-LOOP CONTROL
  • EMBEDDED BUILD

A single-purpose bedside player built around a behavioural problem rather than a technical one. Opening ASMR on a phone at bedtime reliably turns into doomscrolling, so this device holds only a synced personal library and offers no route to anything else. The controls are designed for the dark and half-asleep: slide for position, rotate for volume, pull to commit — one job per physical form, so each is identifiable by touch. The hero control is a motorised fader that both shows and sets playback position. The working prototype closes the loop with 16-bit position feedback and an H-bridge driver and measures ±0.30 mm over 100 mm of travel. The useful finding was where the limit sat: the remaining error is the mechanism settling, not the control loop, so I documented the trade-off either side of the chosen value and stopped there, because the product doesn’t need more. Enclosure, screen interface and sync are still to come.
